Step 1: The Monolithic Unnormalized Form (UNF) & Relational Anomalies
Severe Anomalies Present

Consider a legacy university laboratory spreadsheet tracking equipment assets, custody loans, and service repairs inside a single monolithic relation:

R_UNF = { asset_tag, serial_no, equip_name, model, cost, cat_name, cat_desc, lab_name, room, building, dept_code, dept_name, manager_name, manager_email, supplier_name, supplier_email, supplier_phone, supplier_addr, {borrow_id, user_email, user_name, role, borrow_date, expected_return, actual_return}, {maint_id, tech_name, service_date, maint_type, maint_cost, maint_desc} }
Insertion Anomaly
A newly constructed laboratory room or signed vendor cannot be stored in the database without fabricating a placeholder equipment asset, because asset_tag acts as the primary identifier.
Deletion Anomaly
If the only asset housed in a laboratory is decommissioned and deleted, the entire record of the laboratory, room number, department, and supervisor is unintentionally purged.
Update Anomaly
If a supplier's phone number or a category description changes, hundreds of duplicate equipment rows must be updated individually. Any partial failure produces mutually contradictory data records.
Step 2: Transition to First Normal Form (1NF)
Domain Atomicity Enforced

Rule: All underlying attribute domains must contain strictly atomic (scalar) values. Extract multi-valued repeating groups (nested arrays of borrowings and service history) into distinct relation instances.

1. Equipment 1NF
Candidate Key: {asset_tag}
R_EQ_1NF(asset_tag, serial_no, name, model, cost, cat_name, cat_desc, lab_name, room, building, dept_code, dept_name, supplier_name)
2. Borrowings 1NF
Composite Key: {asset_tag, user_email, borrow_date}
R_BORR_1NF(asset_tag, user_email, borrow_date, user_name, role_name, expected_return, actual_return, purpose)
3. Maintenance 1NF
Composite Key: {asset_tag, service_date, maint_type}
R_MAINT_1NF(asset_tag, service_date, maint_type, tech_name, tech_email, cost, description, status)
Step 3: Transition to Second Normal Form (2NF)
Partial Key Dependencies Removed

Rule: Must be in 1NF and contain no partial functional dependencies. No non-prime attribute may depend on a proper subset of any composite candidate key.

Partial Dependencies Identified in 1NF Borrowings:
Composite Candidate Key K = {asset_tag, user_email, borrow_date}
FD_1: {asset_tag, user_email, borrow_date} → {expected_return, actual_return, purpose} [Full Key Dependency - OK]
FD_2: {user_email} → {user_name, role_name} [PARTIAL DEPENDENCY on proper subset {user_email}]
FD_3: {asset_tag} → {equip_name, cost} [PARTIAL DEPENDENCY on proper subset {asset_tag}]

2NF Decomposition: Extracted partially dependent attributes into separate relations:

users
users(user_id, email, first_name, last_name, role_id, department_id)
borrowings
borrowings(borrowing_id, equipment_id, user_id, borrow_date, expected_return_date, actual_return_date)
Step 4: Transition to Third Normal Form (3NF) & Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF)
Transitive Dependencies Eradicated

3NF Rule: No non-prime attribute may be transitively dependent on any candidate key ($X \to Y$ and $Y \to Z$).
BCNF Rule: For every non-trivial functional dependency $X \to Y$, $X$ must be a superkey.

Transitive Dependencies in 2NF Equipment:
asset_tag → lab_id → department_id → building (Transitive: lab_id is not a candidate key of equipment)
asset_tag → category_id → category_description (Transitive: category_id is not a candidate key of equipment)
